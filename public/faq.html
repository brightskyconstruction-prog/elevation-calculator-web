Elevation & Grade Calculator — FAQ

Common questions about elevation calculation, slope, grade, and surveying for construction

What is an elevation calculator?

An elevation calculator is a tool used in construction and surveying to record, compare, and calculate differences in ground or structure elevation between survey points. It helps construction crews verify grades for drainage, foundations, roads, and pipe laying without manual arithmetic.

How do I calculate elevation grade?

Elevation grade (slope percentage) = (Rise ÷ Run) × 100. Rise is the vertical elevation difference; Run is the horizontal distance between the two points.

Example: Point A at 101.25 ft, Point B at 99.00 ft, 45 ft apart horizontally → grade = (2.25 ÷ 45) × 100 = 5%.

How do I read survey elevations?

Survey elevations are read using a level instrument and a leveling rod (staff). The process:

Height of Instrument (HI) = Benchmark Elevation + Backsight Reading
Derived Elevation = HI − Foresight Rod Reading

This process is repeated at each setup point and is called differential leveling.

What is a benchmark elevation in construction?

A benchmark is a point of known elevation used as a reference for all measurements on a job site. It is typically a permanent marker — a nail in a curb, a brass disk, or a hub — whose elevation has been established by a licensed surveyor. All derived elevations are calculated relative to this benchmark.

What is the difference between slope and grade?

Slope and grade describe the same thing — the steepness of a surface — but are expressed differently:

Grade: percentage (e.g., 5%) — most common in construction
Slope: ratio (1:20), fraction (0.05), or angle in degrees

A 5% grade means the ground rises 5 feet for every 100 feet of horizontal distance.

How do I calculate rod reading from a benchmark?

Step 1: Take a backsight reading on the benchmark.
Step 2: HI = Benchmark Elevation + Backsight Reading
Step 3: Take a foresight rod reading at the unknown point.
Step 4: Derived Elevation = HI − Foresight Reading

Example: Benchmark = 100.00 ft, Backsight = 4.52 ft → HI = 104.52 ft. Foresight at Point A = 6.15 ft → Point A Elevation = 104.52 − 6.15 = 98.37 ft.

What is differential leveling?

Differential leveling is the process of determining the elevation difference between two points using a level instrument and a leveling rod. The instrument is set up between the points; a backsight is taken on the known point and a foresight on the unknown point. The elevation difference = backsight − foresight. Multiple setups are chained together when the distance is too large for one shot.
